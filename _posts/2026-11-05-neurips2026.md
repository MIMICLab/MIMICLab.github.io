Our paper, “PP-Mark: Provable and Publicly Verifiable Watermarking for Generative AI,” has been accepted at NeurIPS 2026.

The paper addresses a fundamental challenge in establishing the authenticity of AI-generated images: how can anyone verify the origin of an image without making the verification system vulnerable to forgery? Existing watermarking methods often rely on private detectors because publicly accessible detection mechanisms can be exploited by adversaries to forge watermarks or manipulate images to pass verification. However, keeping detectors private introduces a centralized trust dependency, preventing independent public verification.

To address this challenge, we propose PP-Mark, a watermarking framework that combines latent-space watermark embedding with zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs). Rather than relying solely on statistical watermark detection, PP-Mark cryptographically binds each watermark to its generation context, including the prompt, model, and random seed. Using the SP1 zero-knowledge proving system, it enables third parties to verify the consistency of the watermark embedding process without revealing the producer's secret key or relying on a centralized verification service.

A key contribution of PP-Mark is its two-layer verification architecture. The statistical detection layer provides efficient watermark screening and remains robust against common image transformations, while the cryptographic verification layer validates an image-bound proof of the embedding process. This combination enables both practical watermark detection and stronger protection against forgery, with formal unforgeability guarantees under explicitly defined cryptographic assumptions.

We evaluated PP-Mark on Stable Diffusion 2.1 and SDXL against representative watermarking methods under black-box imprint forgery, white-box adversarial optimization, and watermark removal attacks. Under the evaluated white-box attacks, existing baseline methods reached a 100% forgery acceptance rate, whereas PP-Mark's proof-based verification rejected all tested forgeries on both models. PP-Mark also maintained a 98% watermark detection rate after diffusion-based regeneration attacks, demonstrating strong resistance to watermark removal while preserving practical detection capabilities.

These results demonstrate that publicly verifiable watermarking need not depend on keeping detection algorithms secret. By combining statistical detection with cryptographic evidence, PP-Mark provides a foundation for decentralized verification of AI-generated content, with potential applications in digital content provenance, synthetic media authentication, and trustworthy generative AI systems.

Congratulations to Hankyeong Ko, the first author of this work, on this achievement. The implementation and reproduction materials are available through our [GitHub repository](https://github.com/hko920920/PP-Mark_NeurIPS2026).
